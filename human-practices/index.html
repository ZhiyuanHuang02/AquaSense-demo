Human practices

Start with the system around the sensor.

A result can create anxiety, cost, and responsibility. AquaSense treats access to confirmation, communication, and remediation as design requirements—not downstream details.

Operating principle
Do not invite a community to detect a problem unless there is a credible path to confirm it and act.

AquaSense is a portfolio concept and has not been co-developed with affected communities. This page documents the questions, roles, and integration method that responsible future engagement would require.

03 · Responsible research

Risks live beyond the reaction.

The strongest failure modes are social as well as technical. Each risk needs an owner and a mitigation that can be evaluated.

!

False reassurance

A low signal could be mistaken for an all-clear despite unmeasured contaminants or a false negative.

Mitigation

Never use “safe”; show the analyte, scope, control state, uncertainty, and confirmation guidance together.

↔

Unequal follow-up

Screening can expose a problem without making certified testing or remediation affordable.

Mitigation

Launch only with funded confirmation and referral pathways defined by local partners.

⌁

Sample location privacy

Location data may identify a household, well owner, or community before they control the narrative.

Mitigation

Collect the minimum metadata, separate public reporting from identifiable records, and define data ownership first.

♻

Material burden

A sealed single-use format improves containment while creating plastic and reagent waste.

Mitigation

Minimize material, evaluate take-back logistics, and compare impacts against alternative screening workflows.

04 · Engagement plan

Ask in the right order.

Future work would pause at explicit decision gates. Technical development does not automatically authorize field deployment.

  1. Listen

    Define the decision

    Meet prospective community partners and ask whether lead screening is a priority, what is already working, and who should own results.

    Gate: shared problem definition
  2. Co-design

    Prototype the full journey

    Test instructions, sample records, result language, referral options, and disposal—not only the cartridge.

    Gate: acceptable workflow
  3. Validate

    Compare against standards

    Evaluate analytical performance with qualified laboratories and usability with representative users.

    Gate: evidence threshold
  4. Govern

    Agree before deploying

    Set data ownership, communication, confirmation funding, incident response, and stop criteria with partners.

    Gate: community authorization
