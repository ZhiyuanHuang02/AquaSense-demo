Project design

A screening tool built around the decision.

AquaSense is a conceptual, cell-free biosensor for preliminary lead screening. The molecular circuit matters—but so do the enclosure, instructions, and next step.

01 · Challenge

Detection is only useful when it leads somewhere.

Water-quality testing often involves a chain of collection, transport, laboratory analysis, interpretation, and communication. Each handoff adds time and can make preliminary investigation harder in remote or resource-constrained settings.

AquaSense focuses on the earliest decision in that chain: does this sample warrant prioritized confirmatory testing? It is deliberately framed as a screening concept rather than a replacement for accredited laboratory methods.

03 · Proposed mechanism

Recognition, then translation.

The proposed architecture separates the molecular event from the interface that communicates it.

Reportervisible output

A lead-responsive regulatory element is proposed as the recognition layer. Binding changes transcriptional control, allowing a reporter reaction to produce a visible signal. In a freeze-dried cell-free format, the required biological machinery would be stored in the cartridge and rehydrated by the sample.

Concept status: this architecture is a literature-informed design direction. AquaSense has not been constructed or experimentally validated.

04 · Cartridge

Containment you can see.

The enclosure is designed as part of the biosafety strategy, not as packaging added later.

A one-way sample port would direct water onto a freeze-dried reaction pad. A separate control window would indicate whether the chemistry activated correctly. The absorbent final layer would retain liquid after use, while a tamper-evident seam would discourage reopening.

05 · Use boundaries

What AquaSense is—and is not.

Designed to
  • Support preliminary field screening
  • Prioritize samples for laboratory testing
  • Communicate a simple, bounded next step
  • Document uncertainty at the point of use
Not designed to
  • Certify that water is safe to drink
  • Replace accredited quantitative analysis
  • Identify every contaminant in a sample
  • Operate without local implementation guidance
